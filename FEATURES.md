# Sweep — features

Everything in the app, grouped by what it helps with. Written from the code, so
it describes what each feature actually does rather than what it was meant to.

---

## Finding things

**Search across five stores**
One search goes to Amazon, Walmart, Best Buy, eBay and Etsy at the same time.
Results come back in one grid ordered by relevance — each store's best match,
then each store's second, so no store gets buried behind another. Results appear
as each store answers rather than waiting for the slowest.

**Store status strip**
Above the results, a chip for every store: a spinner while it's still checking,
a result count when it's done, a warning when it failed. A store with no matches
and a store that broke are shown differently, because they are different facts.

**Top picks**
Three highlights pulled from across every store: **Cheapest**, **Best reviewed**
and **Biggest drop**.

**Skipped stores**
If a store doesn't sell that kind of thing, Sweep says so — "Skipped Etsy, they
don't sell this kind of thing" — instead of quietly returning less.

**Product lookup**
Paste a link from any supported store and Sweep reads the listing: price, list
price, ratings, sales rank, and everything below.

**Compare**
Star results and they line up side by side, sorted by price. The selection lasts
the whole session, so you can search for one thing, star it, search for another,
and still compare the two.

**Search history**
Reopen a recent search without spending another search. Prices are re-read live
when it opens. Free keeps 10, Pro 50, Ultimate 200.

**Similar products**
Cheaper versions of the same thing, drawn from what has already been searched.

## Knowing whether a price is good

**Price history**
Sweep keeps its own record of what every product has cost. It's a record Sweep
watched happen, not a figure the store published about itself.

**Is this sale real?**
The feature Sweep is built around. A store sets the original price and sets the
discount, so a red "40% off" badge is its word for both. Sweep compares the
"sale" against its own history and says whether the discount is genuine or the
price has been sitting there all month. Where there isn't enough history yet, it
says that instead of guessing.

**What buyers actually said**
Real reviews split into what people liked, what they didn't, and what they
disagree about — with buyer photos where the store publishes them. Not a summary
written by a model.

**No AI in the price path**
Every price is read from the store's own listing and never predicted. When a
store doesn't publish something, the section is left empty and the store is
named, rather than filled with something plausible.

## Watching prices

**Price tracking**
Track a product and Sweep checks it for you and tells you when the price drops.
Free tracks 3 products checked twice a day; Pro 20; Ultimate 100 with a faster
schedule. Custom drop thresholds on paid plans.

**Sort and filter tracking**
Sort by best deal, biggest drop since you started watching, newest added,
cheapest or name. Filter by product name or store.

**Notifications feed**
Every price drop is also filed in the app, behind the bell. If notifications are
off or you swiped one away, the record is still there. Notifications stay until
you delete them.

**Deal Radar**
A standing search. Tracking answers "tell me when this gets cheaper"; a radar
answers "tell me when anything matching this shows up under $X" — the question
you have before you've picked a listing. It keeps looking for weeks. Free radars
refresh when you tap, twice a day; Pro and Ultimate re-run in the background.

## Planning what to buy

**Cart across stores**
Collect things from any store you searched or tracked, and see what the whole lot
costs and how that total moves over time. Sweep sells nothing — this is a
staging area, not a checkout.

**Lists**
Gift lists and wishlists with live prices, from any mix of stores, with a running
total. Share one by link. Free has 1 list, Pro 5, Ultimate 20.

**Budget**
Log what you actually spent on shopping, against a monthly budget, by category.
A purchase can be logged in one tap straight from something you were tracking.

## Community

**Deals feed**
Real price drops other people are watching, credited to whoever spotted each one
first. Open to guests, since it's the clearest look at what the app does.

**Leaderboard**
XP and ranks. XP is earned by finding deals — a tracked item dropping below its
own historical average — never by buying anything. It ranks judgement, not
spending, and every point is computed on the server from price history.

## Plans and honesty

**Free, properly**
10 searches and 12 product lookups a day, 3 tracked products, price history,
lists and budgeting. No card, no trial that turns into a charge, nothing expires.

**Pro and Ultimate**
Pro ($5.99/month) raises limits to 75 searches, 30 lookups and 20 tracked
products. Ultimate ($11.99/month) goes to 400 searches, 100 lookups and 100
tracked products, with the fastest checking.

**Why are features limited?**
A page explaining what each part of the app actually costs to run, and what a
subscription pays for. The real reason a free account gets 10 searches rather
than 100.

**Retailer not working?**
A live view of which stores are answering and how fast, so a missing store has a
visible reason.

**One optional ad**
Nothing interrupts you while you use the app. The only ad is one you can choose
to watch for an extra search past the daily limit.

**Promo codes**
Redeem a code for free time on a paid plan.

**Guest mode**
Browse without an account, with a smaller daily allowance.

**English and Spanish**
The whole app is translated.
